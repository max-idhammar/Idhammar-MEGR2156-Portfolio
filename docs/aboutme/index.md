#About Me
My name is Max Idhammar, I am a 21 year old mechanical engineering student at UNC Charlotte. Engineering has always been a big part of my life, since my father was an engineer. I have always been interested in how the moving parts of a machine all work together to accomplish a single task that would otherwise be impossible. However, working as a mechanic truly made it clear to me that mechanical engineering was the only path for me. I got to see the way parts fail and why the fail, I liked to imagine solutions to the parts I saw commonly fail. As I've started working on engineering projects for classes I have come to realize I am a very balanced engineer. I always place importance on being decisive, meaning not getting too stuck on one idea or design and being able to quickly pivot if needed, but only after truly considering the idea. Creativity is something that I also value a lot as an engineer since sometimes solutions are not as obvious as you would like. But having a good idea is only valuable if you have the patience and dedication to methodically design, test, and redesign your idea many many times. During my time at UNC Charlotte I have learned so much about being an engineer and gained a lot of valuable experience helping me become the best engineer I can be. For example our final project in 1202: Introduction to Engineering Practice and Principles, was to work with a group to design and model a product meant for storing toiletries. This project was so helpful because not only did we have to come up with a functional product, we also had to look at every aspect from manufacturing to use. We considered what materials to make the product out of for it to be strong yet portable, we were told to assume we would be using injection molding, so our design had to account for that as well. Projects like these have led me to becoming the best engineer I can be, this year I hope to be challenged like that again so that I can become an even more well-rounded engineer.

"What does it mean to defend an engineering decision : and do you currently know how to do it?" 

Defending an engineering decision means, to me, that you can support your decision with actual calculations and reasoning for why you made that choice. You should be able to point out alternative choices and again with clear calculations and logical reasoning, be able to show why the decision you made is the best choice based on the parameters given.
  I would say I have not yet learned how to fully defend an engineering decision, I do not yet have the knowledge required. However, it is something I know I will need to be able to do before I leave UNC Charlotte.
